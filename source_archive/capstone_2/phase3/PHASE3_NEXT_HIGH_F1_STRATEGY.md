# Phase 3: next high-F1 experiment (plan only)

## What the saved runs establish

- The last locally available executed Sections notebook reached 0.5066 supported macro F1 on the 10-document official development set, but only 0.4551 pooled F1 on its first 16-document held-out training fold. In the fold-0 model, global-head training loss fell from 4.078 to 0.576 over 35 epochs while its official-development score remained near 0.45. It then failed during outer-fold 1 because the old code rebuilt a large recovery ZIP inside distributed training. The subsequent Kaggle status-code-54 fatal session supplied no additional cell logs locally.
- The earlier Context Joint pilot trained the same Phase 1 base 03 end to end for 15 epochs. Its best held-out fold F1 was 0.4660. The training loss fell to 0.038, but development F1 had already peaked at 0.5191 in epoch 10 and fell thereafter. More epochs alone are therefore contradicted by the observed curve.
- That pilot's held-out errors are concentrated in semantic oppositions: ArgumentRespondent F1 was 0.137 (171 of 301 gold respondent sentences were called ArgumentPetitioner); PrecedentNotReliedUpon F1 was 0.065 (79 of 167 were called PrecedentReliedUpon); RulingByLowerCourt F1 was 0.293 (83 of 221 were called RatioOfTheDecision). These are better targets than another generic document head.
- The official data contain 80 training documents and 16,845 labeled sentences, split 40/40 across CL and IT. The official development set has 10 documents and 2,142 sentences. Its small size makes repeated checkpoint/ensemble selection noisy: the One Strong ensemble reported 0.5494 development F1 but scored about 0.494 privately.
- Phase 2 Span V3's useful lesson is staged adaptation of more Phase 1 base 03 layers: its configuration moved from four to twelve unfrozen upper blocks, with checkpointing and a modest learning rate. That was a different prediction task, so its score does not transfer directly to Phase 3.
- Label support is highly uneven. `Dissent` appears in only two training documents and no development document; `PrecedentOverruled` appears in only one training document (three sentences) and no development document. `Issue` has 107 training sentences, and 68 of its 83 training segments are one sentence long. `None` has 146 one-sentence segments out of 202. A decoder strongly favoring long spans can erase these short roles.
- The assignment requires starting from the student's Phase 1 model weights and forbids another pretrained language model or external LLM/API. A new public LegalBERT/DeBERTa checkpoint is not a permissible shortcut. The larger existing Phase 1 base 01 (8 layers, width 768) and the deeper base 03 (24 layers, width 384) can be used as separate branches.

## New architecture

1. **Primary text tower: Phase 1 base 03.** Encode the marked target sentence with up to two neighbors on each side and a 448–512 token budget. Fine-tune more pretrained capacity than the Sections run: LoRA rank 16 on attention projections throughout the 24 blocks, plus full training of the upper 12 blocks initially. If held-out scores still rise, unfreeze the upper 16 in a short second stage. Keep lower-layer learning rates much smaller. This uses existing pretrained capacity rather than adding a large random head.
2. **Sequence head trained with the text tower.** Pool the target tokens, add normalized document position and CL/IT domain embeddings, and process overlapping 12–24 sentence windows with a modest bidirectional recurrent/attention layer. Predict all 13 labels and learn a linear-chain CRF transition model. Average overlapping-window emission scores and decode the whole document. Compare raw emissions and CRF output on held-out documents; retain the decoder only if it helps. Do not impose the previous fixed 64-sentence span prior.
3. **Confusable-label supervision.** In addition to the 13-way loss, train small conditional heads for ArgumentPetitioner vs ArgumentRespondent, PrecedentReliedUpon vs PrecedentNotReliedUpon, and RulingByLowerCourt vs RulingByPresentCourt. Give these low auxiliary weights and use hard examples from the same document. Apply mild class balancing and logit adjustment; avoid repeatedly presenting a single rare-label document as if it were independent evidence.
4. **Optional wider, independent tower: Phase 1 base 01.** Fine-tune its width-768 checkpoint on the same target-aware task. For its causal attention, place both neighboring contexts before the target so the target representation can attend to them. Train and validate it separately. Include it in the final ensemble only if it raises pooled document-held-out F1 in both CL and IT. This is the larger-capacity branch; adding its random parameters to the first tower without validation is not the plan.

## Training and measurement

- Sample domain, then document, then a window. This prevents the 1,816-sentence CL document from dominating optimization. Reserve roughly one third of windows for boundaries, rare labels, and the three confusion pairs; draw the rest uniformly so the full distribution remains represented. Cover all sentences over successive epochs.
- Start with the upper pretrained blocks and regularized heads; use mixed precision, activation checkpointing, token-bucket batches, gradient accumulation, and two-GPU DDP. Use a lower encoder learning rate (order 1e-5) than the heads (order 1e-4), warmup, clipping, EMA or checkpoint averaging, and stop on held-out F1 rather than training loss. The previous 15-epoch curve is a reason to expect the best checkpoint before the final epoch.
- Build five domain-balanced document folds. A fold's complete pipeline must use the same encoder for its training and held-out inference, avoiding the Sections run's mixed cross-fit-feature versus full-model-feature distribution. Pool predictions from all held-out documents and report macro F1, CL/IT F1, each label's precision/recall/F1, and confusion counts. Keep the 10 official development documents for a final independent check rather than tuning every epoch and blend on them.
- Treat `Dissent` and `PrecedentOverruled` separately in reports: their one/two-document support makes any fold estimate unstable. Never call a gain from predicting these classes on one document a reliable general improvement.
- After the primary tower, test class-bias calibration and CRF strength only on pooled out-of-fold predictions, with a small regularized search. Try an equal-weight or lightly optimized blend with the wider tower only after measuring its independent predictions. A minimum promotion condition is roughly **0.55 pooled held-out macro F1**, a clear gain over the 0.455–0.466 pilots, no collapse in either domain, and a consistent official-development check. This gate is evidence for trying a submission, not a guarantee of private 0.8.

## Compute and crash recovery

- Budget approximately 5–6 hours for the primary tower across folds and a full-data fit, 2–3 hours for the wider tower if the primary pilot improves, and the remaining time for calibration, final inference, packaging, and validation. Two GPUs should train the same branch with DDP; run the branches sequentially. Measure actual throughput before committing the optional branch.
- Save model, optimizer, scheduler, scaler, fold assignments, configuration, predictions, metrics, and RNG states atomically every 10–15 minutes. Rebuild ZIP archives only after distributed workers exit. Do not count a checkpoint kept solely in `/kaggle/working` as crash-proof: the fatal draft session showed that it can become inaccessible. Persist periodic recovery archives to a separately saved Kaggle version/dataset or a private user-owned storage repository (storage only, never external pretrained weights), and verify each archive by checksum. If remote write access is unavailable, stop after a completed stage and save/download its output before continuing.

## Expected result and limit

This is a materially different use of compute: pretrained-backbone adaptation, document-balanced supervision, fold-validated model selection, and a conditional wider-model ensemble. The available results do **not** justify promising 0.8. The very small document set and effectively unlearnable rare labels may limit the ceiling; only held-out and private evaluations can establish the attainable score.
